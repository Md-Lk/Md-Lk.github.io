Home

This EF2023 Dashboard is a grouping and visual summary of Canada Energy Regulator’s raw data on future outlooks for Canadian energy. Its purpose is to highlight the projected importance of energy sources across Canada as it adheres to net-zero targets up to 2050.

To focus on energy sources, this rendition tabulates drivers affecting projected importance under demand, availability, trade and emissions1.

  • Demand identifies rising/descending sources, and provides a consumption-side sector view.

  • Availability ranks production by fuel type.

  • Trade lists projected fuel/electricity interchange volumes and pricing dynamics.

  • Emissions overviews sectors’ projected contribution to greenhouse gas emissions (in megatonnes of equivalent CO2).

Takeaway examples, summary statistics, and data dictionaries are provided with every main tab within hoverable sidebars2 or definition boxes.

Context

“What does our journey look like over the next 30 years?”.

— Canada Energy Regulator

The CER published EF2023 data in 2024 as part of the Canada’s Energy Future series in assessment of alignment with Paris Agreement targets3.

In their own words, the dataset “explores how possible energy futures might unfold for Canadians over the long term”. Data covers fuel pricing, energy trade streams, industrial activity, electrical systems, energy demand, and economic indicators.

Below is a digest of data trends to expect in the dashboard, focusing on 2023-2050 projections and attempting to estimate the scale of corrections required to achieve net-zero targets, as well as their consequences.

Likely future scenarios

Note that:

  • Footnotes specify amounts.

  • Unless specified otherwise, values in pairs refer to Canada Net-zero and Global Net-zero scenarios (respectively).

  • Likewise, sets of 4 refer to 2022 values followed by 3 projected differences (2050 values under Current Measures, Canada Net-zero and Global Net-zero scenarios).

Demand

  • Domestic primary demand decreasing partly4.

    • Net-zero demand driven down by Natural Gas plus Refined Petroleum Products5

    • Demand driven up by Landfill Gas and Other Renewables plus Nuclear energy6

  • Domestic secondary demand decreasing7.

    • Demand driven down mainly by Natural Gas8, esp. in Industry.

    • Demand driven up mainly by Electricity, plus Hydrogen9, esp. in Transportation.

Availability

  • Domestic Natural Gas and Crude Oil production decreasing10.

    • Production projected to peak in 2030 or 2026 before decreasing11.

  • Domestic Electricity and Hydrogen production increasing12.

  • High hydrogen production growth13.

Trade

  • Hydrocarbon supply and demand in equilibrium barring Ethane:

    • Domestic Ethane demand projected constant in all scenarios14.

    • Ethane production projected decreasing, requiring imports in Net-zero scenarios15.

  • Global Net-zero Pentanes+ production surplus making it a potential new future export (past 2040).

Emissions

  • ~75% emission cuts between 2022 and 2050 may enable carbon offsets sufficient for Net-zero.

    • Simultaneous reduction and absorption is needed (target at ~165 to 155 MtCO2eq/yr).

    • Top 3 carbon-intensive sectors are Oil and Gas, Transportation, and Buildings (over 60%)16.

    • Top 3 carbon removal pathways involve LULUCF, DAC, and Electricity.

    • Electricity may be carbon-neutral by 2035 (both Net-zero scenarios).
