About

The dashboard and accompanying pages were built in R using Quarto and an R package developed for this purpose. Data source details, the dashboard hierarchy, and a data dictionary are each provided below.

Dataset information

Full dataset name: “Canada’s Energy Future 2023: Energy Supply and Demand Projections to 2050”

Dataset page: https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/7643c948-d661-4d90-ab91-e9ac732fc737

Data source: Open Government (https://open.canada.ca/en)

Record ID: 7643c948-d661-4d90-ab91-e9ac732fc737

Digital Object Identifier (DOI): https://doi.org/10.35002/zppg-yr91

Organization Name: Canada Energy Regulator (CER) / Régie de l’énergie du Canada (REC)

Data dictionary

Demand:

primary_demand

Generation-side energy demand by province (since 2005, estimated, in petajoules)

end_use_demand

Consumption-side energy demand by sector, source and province (since 2005, in petajoules)

Availability:

natural_gas_production

The volume of natural gas produced (since 2005, in 1000000m³/day or 1000000bbl/day)

crude_oil_production

The volume of crude oil production by type and province (since 2005, in 1000m³/day or 1000bbl/day)

hydrogen_production

Hydrogen produced by province and production method (since 2010, in megatonnes)

electricity_capacity

Electricity generation capacity of provinces by plant/fuel type (since 2005, in megawatts)

electricity_generation

Electricity generated across provinces by plant/fuel type (since 2005, in gigawatt-hours)

Trade:

pentanes_plus

The volume of pentane/derivatives across trade streams (since 2010, in 1000m³/day or 1000bbl/day)

ethane

The volume of ethane/derivatives across trade streams (since 2010, in 1000m³/day or 1000bbl/day)

propane

The volume of propane/derivatives across trade streams (since 2010, in 1000m³/day or 1000bbl/day)

butane

The volume of butane/derivatives across trade streams (since 2010, in 1000m³/day or 1000bbl/day)

electricity_interchange

Electricity in/out-flows by province (since 2005, CA-CA or CA-US, in gigawatt-hours)

benchmark_prices

Prices for crude oil and natural gas based on North American benchmarks (since 2005, in various units)

end_use_prices

End-use price by energy source, sector, and province (since 2005, in CAD/gigajoule, 2022-adjusted)

Emissions:

greenhouse_gas_emissions

Greenhouse gas emissions by sector (since 2005, in megatonnes of CO2 equivalent)

Not included:

natural_gas_drilling

A count of natural gas wells drilled by province and method (since 2000)

electricity_capacity_technology1

Energy generation capacity of provinces by method (since 2020, in megawatts)

electricity_generation_technology2

Energy generated across provinces by method (since 2020, in gigawatt-hours)

macro_indicators

Misc. measures of the economy at large (since 2005, in misc. units)

Navigation tips

  • Single- or double-click legend items.

  • Graph zoom is controllable by setting range limits or dragging corner handles (along vertical axes).

  • Hoverable sidebars (the top Summary section on mobile devices) are scrollable and contain tab overviews (takeaway examples, summary statistics, a data dictionary…).

  • (Correction) On stacked column graphs: The “Reset axes” button (top right) reframes views between scenarios as needed3.

Details for interpretation

  • Dates span 2005 and 20504.

  • Throughout the dashboard, as applicable, subcategories are sorted by decreasing maximum quantity. This makes top/bottom categories more visible5, and works in tandem with hoverable range indicators6.

  • Due to low comparability, electricity_capacity_technology and electricity_generation_technology were not used (electricity type counts, grouping criteria, and naming schemes vary from one scenario to the next). Likewise, macro_indicators and natural_gas_drilling were excluded for capturing insights requiring familiarity with e.g., Kaya identity or with the impact of the 2008 oil glut on oil well counts (which don’t appear to vary across scenarios in any case.).

Strengths and weaknesses

Inspired by CER’s EF2023 data exploration tools and resources, this dashboard was made to emphasize future energy the sources themselves (as seen in facets and legend groups). As a result, some of the gaps found to stand in the way of insight extraction or big-picture understanding in these resources are hopefully complemented by this work.

A list of main, known advantages and shortcomings introduced is provided below.

  • The nested hierarchy promotes a more coherent structure. This reflects the following logical progression by design. The relevance of future energy sources7 starts with Demand. It must therefore be covered by Supply, which motivates Trade streams as per pricing dynamics. This results in Emissions, which is monitored across sectors. This exact progression is reflected across tabs to make this layout easier to digest.

  • Components in each view are ranked by default. This means that top/bottom subcategories are more obvious at first glance.

  • View switching is more convenient; there is no disruption from loading screens shown the first time a view switches.

  • The scale of changes is easier to compare across scenarios; axes are not rescaled as frequently herein.

As for drawbacks, these include:

  • Plots are less mobile-friendly8.

  • View switching being less visually dynamic (slicers use method=“update” in plotly).

  • Potentially more data being cached by the browser per view (CER spreads data subsets more carefully between slices).

Finally, a more neutral, and somewhat subjective difference is that plot variants are more linear here, and are subject to fewer variations. Depending on readers, this may be found to enhance the layout logic and ease of exploration, or to reduce the freedom of interpretability or visual appeal. In an effort to reconcile these, custom visualization functions were developed and maintained throughout this project9.
